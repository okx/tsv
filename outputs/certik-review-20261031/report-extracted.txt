                                                                                                          APPROACH & METHODS        OKX TSV - AUDIT


APPROACH & METHODS                                         OKX TSV - AUDIT



This audit was conducted for OKX to evaluate the security and correctness of the smart contracts associated with the OKX
TSV - Audit project. The assessment included a comprehensive review of the in-scope smart contracts. The audit was
performed using a combination of Manual Review and Static Analysis.

The review process emphasized the following areas:

   Architecture review and threat modeling to understand systemic risks and identify design-level flaws.

   Identification of vulnerabilities through both common and edge-case attack vectors.

   Manual verification of contract logic to ensure alignment with intended design and business requirements.

   Assessment of code quality and maintainability, including adherence to current best practices and industry
   standards.


The audit resulted in findings categorized across multiple severity levels, from informational to critical. To enhance the
project’s security and long-term robustness, we recommend addressing the identified issues and considering the following
general improvements:

   Improve code readability and maintainability by adopting a clean architectural pattern and modular design.

   Strengthen testing coverage, including unit and integration tests for key functionalities and edge cases.

   Maintain meaningful inline comments and documentations.

   Implement clear and transparent documentation for privileged roles and sensitive protocol operations.

   Regularly review and simulate contract behavior against newly emerging attack vectors.

  Overview

The OKX TSV project is a permissioned stock venue on Uniswap v4. In-scope components are                     TSVRegistryV1     ,

 TSVHookV1    , TSVSwapRouterV1      , TSVLPManagerV1      , TSVVolumeLedgerV1       , and  ERC8056DirectLegalShareAdapter          .
Swaps and liquidity are executed by the external Uniswap v4           PoolManager     , which calls back into   TSVHookV1    . Participant
identity is read from an external SBT. Each pool pairs a permissions-adapter stock token with an external quote ERC-20. The
stock adapter reads an external issuer allowlist checker, and legal-share accounting reads the underlying token's

 uiMultiplier     and   decimals    . TSVLPManagerV1       also depends on external Permit2, WETH9, and a position descriptor.

  Privileged Functions

In the OKX TSV project, privileged roles are used to support dynamic runtime updates. These roles are described in the

 Centralization Related Risks            and   Centralized Control of Contract Upgrade                 findings. The advantage of those
privileged roles in the codebase is that the client reserves the ability to adjust the protocol according to the runtime required
to best serve the community. It is also worth noting the potential drawbacks of these functions, which should be clearly stated
through the client's action/plan. Additionally, if the private keys of the privileged accounts are compromised, it could lead to
devastating consequences for the project.

To improve the trustworthiness of the project, dynamic runtime updates in the project should be notified to the community.
Any plan to invoke the aforementioned functions should be also considered to move to the execution queue of the

 Timelock     contract.

  Recommendation

The finding reflects the current project design and may require iterative improvements to operational security and
decentralization. In most cases, these risks cannot be fully resolved at the present stage. We advise the client to securely
manage the private keys associated with privileged accounts to reduce the risk of compromise. In general, we strongly

recommend that centralized privileges or roles be managed through decentralized mechanisms or secure smart-contract-
based accounts, such as multisignature wallets.

The following are feasible measures that may mitigate these risks in the short and long term or, where applicable,
permanently resolve them:


Short Term:

Timelock and Multi sign (⅔, ⅗) combination mitigate by delaying the sensitive operation and avoiding a single point of key

   Time-lock with reasonable latency, e.g., 48 hours, for awareness on privileged operations;
   AND

   Assignment of privileged roles to multi-signature wallets to prevent a single point of failure due to the private key

   AND

   A medium/blog link for sharing the timelock contract and multi-signers addresses information with the public

Long Term:

Timelock and DAO, the combination, mitigate by applying decentralization and transparency.

   Time-lock with reasonable latency, e.g., 48 hours, for awareness on privileged operations;

In the UUPS-upgradeable contracts    TSVRegistryV1    , TSVHookV1   , TSVVolumeLedgerV1    , TSVLPManagerV1   , and

 TSVSwapRouterV1   , any account holding  UPGRADE_ADMIN_ROLE      in TSVRegistryV1    can authorize upgrades to the
implementations used by their proxies.

For  TSVRegistryV1   , upgrade authorization is implemented through   _authorizeUpgrade()      in TSVGoverned   , which
directly checks UPGRADE_ADMIN_ROLE    :


  function _authorizeUpgrade(address) internal view virtual override {
      if (!hasRole(UPGRADE_ADMIN_ROLE, msg.sender)) {
          revert UnauthorizedUpgrade();
      }
  }


The remaining business contracts query the same role from     TSVRegistryV1   . For example,  TSVVolumeLedgerV1
implements the following authorization check:


  function _authorizeUpgrade(address) internal view override {
      if (!registry.hasRole(UPGRADE_ADMIN_ROLE, msg.sender)) {
          revert Unauthorized();
      }
  }


The project deploys these contracts through   TSVERC1967Proxy   , an ERC-1967 proxy intended for use with their UUPS
upgrade implementations:


  contract TSVERC1967Proxy is ERC1967Proxy {
      constructor(address implementation, bytes memory data)
          ERC1967Proxy(implementation, data)
      {
          if (data.length == 0) revert EmptyInitializationData();
      }
  }

The Registry initializer grants both       DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE         and   UPGRADE_ADMIN_ROLE          to the initial administrator. A holder of

 DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE         can also grant     UPGRADE_ADMIN_ROLE          to another account:

  _grantRole(UPGRADE_ADMIN_ROLE, admin);


Any compromise of an         UPGRADE_ADMIN_ROLE         holder could allow an attacker to upgrade the proxies to malicious
implementations and execute arbitrary logic through them. This could result in bypassed asset-admission and participant-
permission checks, manipulated pool lifecycle and volume records, disabled quota enforcement, redirected swap
settlements, altered LP positions or forced-exit behavior, theft of assets held by or approved to protocol components, or full
compromise of the protocol’s business contracts.


  Recommendation

We recommend that the team make efforts to restrict access to the admin of the proxy contract. A strategy of combining a
time-lock and a multi-signature (⅔, ⅗) wallet can be used to prevent a single point of failure due to a private key
compromise. In addition, the team should be transparent and notify the community in advance whenever they plan to migrate
to a new implementation contract.

Here are some feasible short-term and long-term suggestions that would mitigate the potential risk to a different level and
suggestions that would permanently fully resolve the risk.


Short Term:

A combination of a time-lock and a multi signature (⅔, ⅗) wallet mitigate the risk by delaying the sensitive operation and
avoiding a single point of key management failure.

   A time-lock with reasonable latency, such as 48 hours, for awareness of privileged operations;
   AND

   Assignment of privileged roles to multi-signature wallets to prevent a single point of failure due to a private key
   compromised;
   AND

   A medium/blog link for sharing the time-lock contract and multi-signers addresses information with the community.


For remediation and mitigated status, please provide the following information:

   Provide the deployed time-lock address.


   Provide the gnosis address with ALL the multi-signer addresses for the verification process.


   Provide a link to the medium/blog with all of the above information included.



Long Term:

A combination of a time-lock on the contract upgrade operation and a DAO for controlling the upgrade operation mitigate the
contract upgrade risk by applying transparency and decentralization.

OTA-15              Lack Of A Storage Gap Or Namespaced Storage Layout In An
                    Upgradeable Contract




 Category                    Severity                          Location                                                    Status


 Design Issue                    Informational                 governance/TSVGoverned.sol: 9                                   Pending


  Description


 TSVGoverned      is an upgradeable base contract inherited by          TSVRegistryV1     . Although future implementations may extend

 TSVGoverned      with additional state variables, the contract neither reserves a storage gap nor places its state in a dedicated
namespaced storage layout.

In Solidity's inherited storage layout, state variables introduced into a base contract are placed before the state variables
declared by its child contracts. If a future upgrade adds state variables to          TSVGoverned     , the storage slots expected by

 TSVRegistryV1      may shift from their original positions.

The upgraded implementation could consequently read existing Registry state from incorrect slots or overwrite previously
stored values. This may corrupt component addresses, security and token configuration, pool records, permission settings,
lifecycle state, or other Registry data maintained through the proxy.


  Recommendation

To mitigate this issue:

1. For enhanced flexibility in future upgrades of the logic contract, it is prudent to reserve a storage gap of an
   appropriate size in the base contract. This is achieved by declaring a fixed-size array, typically of                  uint256
   elements, each occupying a 32-byte slot, in the base contract. Label this array with the identifier                    __gap     or any
   name prefixed with       __gap_     to indicate its purpose as a reserved space clearly.

2. Alternatively, use an ERC-7201 namespaced storage layout by placing the contract's state variables in one or more
   dedicated storage structs.


For more information: https://docs.openzeppelin.com/contracts/5.x/upgradeable

OTA-16               Forced Exit May Cause LP Loss




 Category               Severity                    Location                                                                       Status


 Design Issue                Informational          periphery/TSVLPManagerV1.sol: 139~164, 174~201                                     Pending


  Description


 unwindPosition       is initiated by the owner of a verified adapter, and         unwindPositionByTsv         is initiated by

 FORCE_EXIT_ADMIN_ROLE         . Both entries take     amount0Min      and   amount1Min      from that caller and pass them into a full-
position   BURN_POSITION      . Neither entry has a deadline. A zero minimum disables that side's check. A nonzero minimum
bounds only principal after fees.

On success the position NFT is burned, and the liquidity is converted once, at the pool price at execution, into both assets
and delivered to the LP. A minimum that is too low still lets that fill succeed. The LP receives the asset mix produced at the
unfavorable price, and the value received can be materially below what the position could have returned at a normal price.
The position no longer exists, so the LP cannot reject the fill or exit again under the LP's own minimums.


  Recommendation

The current design gives the authorized forced-exit caller control over              amount0Min      and   amount1Min     . Operators should
derive these values from recent, independently verified price data and the position’s expected principal outputs, applying
documented slippage limits before executing a forced exit.

If enforceable LP protection is required, add an execution deadline and an on-chain price-deviation or minimum-value
constraint. Operator-supplied minimum amounts alone do not protect the LP against a compromised or negligent forced-exit
operator.

OTA-12              Discussion On Underlying Token Decimals
